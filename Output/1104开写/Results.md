# Results

Confirmatory factor analysis was first conducted to evaluate the measurement model. The CFA demonstrated excellent model fit (χ² = 273.39, df = 160, p < .001; CFI = .91; TLI = .90; RMSEA = .06, 95% CI [.05, .07]; SRMR = .06), with all factor loadings statistically significant and ranging from β = .43 to β = .87. Reliability assessment using both Cronbach's alpha and omega coefficients indicated all constructs exceeded α = .60: Attitude (α = .64, ω = .65), Behavioral Intention (α = .74, ω = .74), Self-efficacy (α = .82, ω = .82), Trust (α = .81, ω = .81), and Health Consciousness (α = .74, ω = .75). Convergent validity was supported through average variance extracted (AVE) values ranging from .35 to .60, with Self-efficacy demonstrating strong convergent validity (AVE = .60), while other constructs showed adequate albeit lower values (Intention AVE = .49, Trust AVE = .46, Attitude AVE = .39, Health Consciousness AVE = .35).

Descriptive statistics revealed that all study variables demonstrated reasonable variability across 199 participants. Attitude (M = 6.11, SD = 0.61), Trust (M = 5.61, SD = 0.69), Self-efficacy (M = 5.53, SD = 0.92), Health Consciousness (M = 5.77, SD = 0.58), and Behavioral Intention (M = 5.83, SD = 0.68) were all measured on 7-point scales. Zero-order correlations demonstrated a notably strong relationship between Attitude and Behavioral Intention (r = .76, p < .001), whereas Information Source showed minimal correlation with Behavioral Intention (r = -.01, ns). Health Consciousness was moderately correlated with both Attitude (r = .53) and Behavioral Intention (r = .56), whereas Message Frame demonstrated weak correlations across most variables.

The structural model examining direct effects revealed that Information Source did not significantly predict Behavioral Intention (β = .02, SE = .07, p = .76), nor did Message Frame (β = .03, SE = .06, p = .67), thus failing to support Hypotheses 1 and 2. However, Attitude emerged as a dominant predictor of Behavioral Intention (β = .75, SE = .07, p < .001), accounting for the majority of explained variance. Self-efficacy served as a secondary predictor (β = .13, SE = .04, p = .001), while Trust demonstrated no significant association with Behavioral Intention (β = .03, SE = .06, p = .65).

Regarding the proposed mediating mechanisms, Information Source significantly influenced Trust (β = -.50, SE = .09, p < .001) and Self-efficacy (β = -.30, SE = .13, p = .02), while demonstrating minimal influence on Attitude (β = .05, SE = .09, p = .59). Message Frame showed weak effects on Attitude (β = .09, SE = .09, p = .31), Self-efficacy (β = .08, SE = .13, p = .51), and Trust (β = .07, SE = .09, p = .46). Despite these upstream effects, indirect pathways remained non-significant. The indirect effect of Information Source through Attitude was non-significant (β = .04, 95% CI: -.09, .16, p = .58), as was the pathway through Trust (β = -.01, 95% CI: -.07, .04, p = .64) and Self-efficacy (β = -.04, 95% CI: -.09, .02, p = .29). Similarly, the indirect effect of Message Frame through Attitude was non-significant (β = .07, 95% CI: -.06, .21, p = .31), through Trust (β = .004, 95% CI: -.02, .03, p = .71), and through Self-efficacy (β = .01, 95% CI: -.02, .04, p = .55). The total indirect effects for both Information Source (β = .02, 95% CI: -.14, .15, p = .94) and Message Frame (β = .08, 95% CI: -.06, .23, p = .31) were non-significant, thus failing to support Hypotheses 3 through 6.

Health Consciousness demonstrated a significant moderating effect on the total effect of Information Source on Behavioral Intention (β = -.39, SE = .14, p = .005), supporting Hypothesis 7. This moderation operated at the indirect pathway level, indicating that Health Consciousness did not moderate the direct Source-Intention relationship (β = .0007, p = .99), but rather moderated how Information Source influenced Behavioral Intention through the proposed mediating mechanisms. Individuals with higher Health Consciousness (1 SD above the mean) exhibited substantially reduced source-dependent variation in behavioral intention, suggesting that health-conscious individuals were less susceptible to source-based persuasion mechanisms operating through cognitive pathways.

The structural model accounted for 56.7% of the variance in Behavioral Intention (R² = .57), representing substantial explanatory power for the outcome variable. Model fit indices suggested areas for structural refinement, though the measurement model demonstrated excellent fit, indicating that the constructs themselves were well-measured but their hypothesized causal interrelationships required reconsideration.
